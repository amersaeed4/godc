signs:
  title: Early Signs of Clinical & Critical Care Nutrition
  text: |-
    Early signs that you may need clinical and critical care nutrition include unexplained weight loss, poor appetite, persistent fatigue, muscle weakness, and difficulty eating or swallowing. These symptoms may indicate that your body is not receiving the nutrients needed for recovery.

    Individuals with serious illnesses, major surgeries, infections, or prolonged hospital stays may also experience increased nutritional needs. Without proper nutritional support, recovery can become slower and more challenging.

    Recognizing these signs early allows healthcare professionals to provide timely nutritional intervention. A personalized nutrition plan can help improve strength, support healing, and reduce the risk of medical complications.

conditions:
  - title: Sepsis & Trauma Nutrition
    text: |-
      Patients with sepsis or severe trauma may have increased nutritional needs during critical illness. Our nutrition support focuses on assessing nutritional status and providing an individualized plan based on the patient’s medical condition, energy needs, fluid requirements and ability to tolerate nutrition.

      Depending on the clinical situation, nutrition may be provided through oral, enteral or parenteral routes. The plan is monitored and adjusted according to the patient’s condition and response to treatment, with the aim of supporting nutritional adequacy throughout critical care and recovery.
    image: nutition1.jpg
  - title: Post-Surgical Nutrition
    text: |-
      Nutrition plays an important role before and after major surgery, particularly when a patient is nutritionally at risk or unable to meet nutritional needs through normal food intake. We assess nutritional status and develop individualized nutrition support according to the type of surgery, medical condition and recovery needs.

      When appropriate, nutritional support may include oral or enteral nutrition, with parenteral nutrition considered when adequate nutrition cannot be achieved through the digestive system. Nutrition is reviewed throughout recovery and adjusted according to the patient’s changing needs.
    image: post-sugical.jpg
  - title: Organ Failure Nutrition Support
    text: |-
      Patients with organ failure often require carefully planned nutrition because their energy, protein, fluid and electrolyte requirements can vary according to their condition and treatment. Our approach focuses on assessing the patient’s nutritional status and developing nutrition support that considers the affected organ and overall clinical condition.

      Nutrition plans are monitored regularly to help maintain adequate nutritional intake while considering factors such as fluid balance, electrolytes and disease-specific requirements. Where needed, enteral or parenteral nutrition can be considered as part of a medically supervised nutrition plan.
    image: organfailure.jpg
  - title: TPN (Total Parenteral Nutrition)
    text: |-
      Total Parenteral Nutrition (TPN) provides nutrients directly into the bloodstream when a patient cannot receive adequate nutrition through the digestive system. It can provide nutrients such as carbohydrates, amino acids, fats, vitamins, minerals and electrolytes according to individual clinical requirements.

      TPN requires careful clinical planning and monitoring, including nutritional requirements, fluid and electrolyte balance, venous access and potential complications. The nutrition plan is individualized and reviewed as the patient’s condition and ability to tolerate other forms of nutrition change.
